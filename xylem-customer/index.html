Three stops · click the one you need

Implement. Operate. Maintain.

A customer can do these without a technician on site. They are not an IFU. They are not a product procedure. They are the stops I would teach first.

  • Before you power it.

    Confirm three things. If any one is missing, do not power the panel. Call the number on the current sheet.

    1. The panel in front of you is the unit on this week’s assignment — not the spare in the shed, not the neighbor’s yard.
    2. The current sheet is with the panel, in the door. Not last year’s printout. Not a photo of a photo.
    3. The stop label and the alarm label on the door match the sheet in the door. If they do not match, stop.

    SAMPLE. Generic yard panel. Not a product model. Not a wiring how-to.

  • The daily read.

    The display is for the daily read — not for troubleshooting. You look once. You write what you see. You do not guess.

    1. What the display is for: today’s status line, as the current sheet names it.
    2. What “normal” looks like: the line the sheet calls normal. If you do not have that line, you do not have a daily read.
    3. Who you call if it is not: the contact on the current sheet. Not a colleague who “knows these.” Not last year’s number.

    SAMPLE. Not a live reading. Not a claim about a real plant.

  • Alarm light on.

    Stop. Do not keep running it to “see if it clears.” Do not improvise a bypass. Do not work off last year’s printout.

    1. Stop. Leave the panel as you found it, except you do not power through an alarm.
    2. Use the current sheet in the door. If it is missing, that is already a stop.
    3. Escalate to the contact on the current sheet. Say: assigned unit, what the display showed, that the alarm light is on, what you did not do (no bypass).

    SAMPLE. Public-safe. Not an IFU. Not a repair.
