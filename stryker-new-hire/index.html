Instructional design sample · week one · mixed-readiness field new hire

Week one. Three stops until a trainer signs you off.

I made this so a new hire and a trainer do not need two different lessons. Same page. Two views. I am Virgil Renfroe. This is a sample I wrote — public-safe stops for a regulated product, not a device IFU, not a Stryker SOP.

If you have done this kind of work before, you still take this version. Prior experience is not the sign-off. The assignment is.

New-hire path · version 1.1 live

Three stops. Then someone signs you off.

You support a regulated product. Until a trainer signs you off on this assignment, these are the stops. Not last year’s habit. Not a product you used somewhere else.

  1. Stop 1 · operate / demonstrate Do not operate or demonstrate a product you have not been assigned. Do not in-service others on it.
  2. Stop 2 · improvise Do not improvise a repair, substitute a part, or work off last year’s printout. Use the current Instructions for Use.
  3. Stop 3 · escalate Stop and escalate: compromised packaging, missing IFU, a request outside your assigned scope.

Mixed readiness

Prior experience is not the sign-off. The assignment is. You still take this version.

Three-item check

Click what you would do. You get a sentence, not a score.

Operate / demonstrate. A customer asks you to power on a product that is not on your assignment. A colleague asks you to in-service it for the room.

Improvise. The current IFU is not in the kit. Last year’s printout is in the van. The part you need is close enough.

Escalate. Packaging is torn. The IFU is missing. Someone asks you to keep going, outside your assigned scope.
