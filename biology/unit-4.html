Unit 4 · Food to work · ~35 minutes

Energy in living things

Food is chemical energy. Cells transform it into a form they can spend. Not an “energy boost.” Not a diet plan.

3 objectives Trace + strip 1 homework

What you'll do

In this sitting:

  1. Trace a short chain from a named meal to a named movement as a transformation, not as “fuel slogans.”
  2. State, conservatively, that plants capture light to build sugars, and that cells break food molecules to capture spendable energy.
  3. Refuse “boost,” “burn fat as a personality,” and medical or diet advice dressed as biology.

Lesson

Energy is not a mood

In ordinary talk, energy is how you feel at 3 p.m. In this course, energy is the capacity to do work — to move something, to build a molecule, to keep a difference across a membrane. Food is not a slogan. It is a set of molecules whose chemical arrangement stores energy that can be released in controlled steps. A sandwich is not “energy” until a body has broken it into pieces a cell can handle and those pieces have been run through reactions that capture some of what was stored.

“I ate for energy” is fine as a calendar note. It is not a mechanism. A mechanism names the transformation. Starch and other carbohydrates can be broken toward simple sugars. Proteins can be broken toward amino acids. Fats can be broken toward pieces that also enter later steps. Those pieces are not yet a stair-climb. In cells, a further set of reactions captures some of the released energy in a small spendable molecule textbooks call ATP. You do not need the full pathway. You need the idea of a coin the cell can spend on pumps, on building, on the proteins that slide and make a muscle shorten. The sandwich became work when that coin was spent. Heat is released along the way. Living things are not perfect banks.

Energy is not created in the cell. It is transformed. That sentence is older than this course and more reliable than a sports drink ad. If a label says “gives you energy,” ask: from what molecules, into what spendable form, to do what work? If the ad cannot answer, it is a tidy made-up reason about feeling awake.

Light, sugar, then everyone else

Plants, algae, and some bacteria capture light. Chlorophyll and related pigments absorb some wavelengths (Unit 1’s green-leaf chain). That captured energy is used in reactions that build sugars from carbon dioxide and water, and oxygen is released as part of that work. This course will not write the full photosynthetic pathway. The system fact is enough: light energy becomes chemical energy stored in sugar. A plant still has mitochondria. It still spends coin to live at night and to build itself. Capturing light does not make a plant an exception to food-to-work. It gives the plant a way to make the food.

Animals, including you, do not run that light-to-sugar step. We eat someone else’s sugar, starch, fat, and protein — a plant, or an animal that ate a plant, or a fungus that ate what was left. Then we run the breakdown-and-capture steps. A cow is not a magic converter. It is a long digestive system and a lot of cells doing the same conservative chemistry. When you climb stairs after lunch, you are not proving a wellness brand. You are spending coin that came from a meal and from stores your cells already held.

Oxygen, and what we will not claim

Many of the capture steps in your cells use oxygen. That is why breathing is not a spiritual practice in this file; it is a supply line. If oxygen is short, some cells can still run a shorter set of reactions for a while, with different leftovers. That is as far as this course will go. We will not turn it into a training plan. We will not tell you how to breathe for performance. We will not diagnose why you got winded. Unit 7 will treat pulse-after-stairs as a trade you can observe, not as a score.

We will also not do diet advice. Calories on a label are a crude measure of how much energy a food could yield if fully used. Bodies are not labels. This course will not tell you what to eat, what to cut, or how to “boost metabolism.” “Metabolism” in a gen-ed sentence means the set of chemical transformations a living thing runs. It is not a personality. It is not a product.

A first-year paper likes to end energy units with “and that is why we must save the rainforest.” You may care about forests. Care is not a chain from sandwich to stair. If you want a public stake, wait for Unit 6 and name a place. Here, stay with the transformation. Name the meal. Name the work. Name the heat that leaked. If you cannot, you are still in the ad.

Practice will ask you to pick the chain that actually transforms something, and to strip a boost slogan. Homework is one food-to-work paragraph about a meal you actually ate and a movement you actually did. If you cannot climb stairs, pick a movement you already do — standing up, walking a hall, lifting a book. Do not start an exercise program for this class. Do not write a protocol for burning anything.

Practice

Strip the boost

Ad version: “This drink boosts your energy so your body can burn fat and live its best life.”

Rewrite as a food-to-work chain. Name molecules or a meal, a spendable form, and a work. No boost, no best life, no diet plan, no medical claim.

Homework

Copy this out. The page won't keep it. 150–250 words.

Name a meal you actually ate and a movement you already do (a hall walk, stairs you already climb, standing from a chair). Trace food → spendable form → work + heat. Then write three sentences this unit refuses: an ad, a diet rule, and a “boost.” Say why each is not a mechanism. Do not prescribe food or exercise. Do not diagnose fatigue. Conceptual only.
