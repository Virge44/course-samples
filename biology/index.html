Household observation only. No invented labs, protocols, or credentials. Draft sample. Do not apply.

Look at a leaf, a bird, your own pulse. Name a part. Say what it does to another part. Say what would come out different if that failed. Window is allowed. No lab coat.

Eight short units. Look first. Then write. About four to six hours. Course samples

What this course will not do

Conceptual only. No pathogen work. No lab protocols for microbes. No culturing, plating, or “grow your own mold” instructions. No genome-editing how-to. No CRISPR steps. No synthesis, reverse genetics, or enhancement. No medical advice — pulse-after-stairs is an observation of a trade, not a diagnosis. If a prompt could be read as a protocol, skip it and use a household observation you can see without making anything grow.

After eight units

  1. Tell a just-so story from a mechanism, and rewrite a purpose-sentence so it names parts, an interaction, and a checkable consequence.
  2. Treat a cell as a bounded system with jobs and traffic, not a bag of vocabulary words.
  3. Say in plain language what DNA and proteins do — information and work — without a genome-editing fantasy.
  4. Trace energy from food to work as a transformation, not a vibe about “boosting.”
  5. State evolution as a tested claim: variation, selection, time — not “the strongest survive.”
  6. Describe organisms in a real place, with constraints, not a poster about “the balance of nature.”
  7. Read the human body as a set of trades (homeostasis), not a machine that should maximize everything at once.
  8. Write a short evidence note from a public-domain teaching figure or a household observation a classmate could refuse.
