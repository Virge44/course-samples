## How to run it

Keep the folder together. Point students at `index.html` opened in a desktop browser, not a Drive preview. Practices are in-page JavaScript. Nothing is uploaded. Homework is not stored in the files — collect it in your LMS journal, a shared folder, or on paper.

Sequence is the course. Eight units, in order. Each sitting: lesson a tired first-year can finish, clickable practice with feedback, one turn-in. Total student time if they do the practices: about four to six hours. This is a compressed 101, not a lab course and not fifteen weeks of coverage. If you stretch it across a term, use the units as weekly spines and add *your* assigned readings. Do not add lab protocols to these files.

## Safety — do not assign as a lab

- No pathogen work. No culturing, plating, incubating, or “grow mold on purpose.”
- No genome-editing how-to. No CRISPR steps. No synthesis, reverse genetics, or enhancement.
- No medical advice. Pulse-after-stairs is an observation of a trade, not a fitness or health test.
- Bread mold, if used: look at mold that already appeared on kitchen bread, through a closed bag if possible. Do not sniff. Do not open a science project. Students who should not handle mold use the bird-at-feeder look or the teaching figure in Unit 8.
- If a student writes a protocol, send it back. The course is conceptual.

## What to collect

| Unit | Turn-in | What to skim for (you do not need a rubric the first time) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Rewrite of a just-so story + one paragraph | Parts, interaction, a consequence that could be wrong. Flag “so that,” “nature wants,” “the body knows.” |
| 2 | One cell job as a system, 150–250 words | Membrane or traffic named. A job that depends on another job. Not a vocabulary list. |
| 3 | Roommate paragraph: DNA and proteins | What the molecule *does*. Flag “blueprint of life,” editing fantasies, CRISPR. |
| 4 | Food → work chain, 150–250 words | Transformation, not “energy boost.” A named meal and a named movement. |
| 5 | Variation / selection / time on one example | All three pieces. Flag “the strongest survive,” purpose-talk. |
| 6 | A real place they can see | Constraints and relationships. Flag “balance of nature,” “the ecosystem wants.” |
| 7 | One bodily trade | Variable in range + what is spent. Not a diagnosis. Not “boost immunity.” |
| 8 | Evidence writeup, 250–400 words | Claim, observation, what would count against it. Household look or teaching figure. No protocol. |

If there is no LMS, a shared doc per student is enough. Grade the public habit (a chain a stranger could refuse), not fluency-as-virtue and not “liking science.”

## Common snags

- Treating Unit 1 as a warmup and writing purpose-stories anyway. Send them back. The explanation type *is* the course.
- Unit 3 becoming a genome-editing essay. Refuse the costume. What the molecule does, not how to change it.
- Unit 5 as “survival of the fittest” plus a nature documentary. Variation, selection, time. Named.
- Unit 7 as WebMD. Conceptual trades only. No treatment talk.
- Unit 8 as a kitchen lab. Observation of what is already there, or the teaching figure. No culturing.
- Students or colleagues asking if he taught biology at A&T. Say: no. Lecturer courses were writing, humanities, and reasoning. *Scientific Development & Social Change* was online course design/ops.

## Optional live / hybrid 20 minutes

Only if a cohort exists. Three volunteers read their Unit 1 rewrite. Class says whether it names parts, an interaction, and a check. One Unit 8 observation pointed at (feeder, stairs, or the figure). Stop. Do not turn it into a lab briefing.

## Accessibility

Pages are keyboardable (skip link, buttons, textareas). Practices give feedback in-page, not only as color. If you need a no-JavaScript path, the homework prompts still function as writing assignments; the clickable practice will not.
