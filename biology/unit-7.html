Unit 7 · Homeostasis as trades · ~40 minutes

The human body as a set of trades

Keep a variable in a range by spending something else. Not a machine that should maximize everything. Not medical advice.

3 objectives Name the trade 1 homework

Not a clinic

This unit is conceptual. It will not tell you what a number should be, whether you are healthy, or what to take, eat, or do about a symptom. Pulse after stairs is an observation of a trade, not a fitness test and not a diagnosis. If you have a reason not to climb stairs, do not. Use a look you already do — standing up, walking a hall — or skip the body observation and use the teaching figure in Unit 8.

Learning objectives

By the end of this unit you will be able to:

  1. Define homeostasis, modestly: keeping an internal variable in a livable range as the outside changes.
  2. Name a trade: what is being kept, and what is spent or risked to keep it (heat vs water, fuel vs work, speed vs accuracy is more than we need — stay with heat, water, fuel).
  3. Refuse “the body knows,” “boost immunity,” and any sentence that turns a trade into a treatment plan.

Lesson

A range, not a statue

Your insides are not the same as the room. Temperature, water, and the availability of sugar in the blood are kept in ranges while the room changes. That keeping is what this course will mean by homeostasis. It is not a statue of perfect balance. It is work. It costs. Unit 4 already said energy is spent. This unit says: some of that spending is the price of staying in range.

“The body knows” is a just-so story with a nervous system drawn on it. Sensors and responses exist. A rise in skin temperature and a set of responses that increase sweating and blood flow near the skin can dump heat. That is a chain. It is not knowledge in the folk sense. If the air is already wet, evaporation slows (Unit 1). The response is still running; the cooling fails. A mechanism that can fail is a better sentence than a body that “knows.”

You do not need a map of every organ. You need one variable, one response, and one way the response costs something. That is the trade.

Three ordinary trades

Heat versus water. Sweat can cool you because evaporation takes heat from the skin. Water left the body to buy that cooling. In dry air the purchase works better. In humid air you spend water and get less cooling. You cannot maximize cooling and water retention at once in a hot, wet room. That is a trade, not a failure of character.

Fuel versus work. Climbing stairs you already climb, your muscles spend the coin from Unit 4. Breathing and pulse often rise because more blood is being moved and more oxygen is being supplied to those cells. That is not a score. It is a reallocation: more of the body’s current spending goes to movement and to keeping oxygen and fuel arriving. Sit still afterward and the allocation can shift back. If you write “this means I am out of shape” or “this means I am fine,” you have left the course and entered a magazine.

Water versus salt and volume, kept light. Thirst and the way the kidneys handle water (we will not write a protocol or a drug) are part of keeping volume and concentration in range. A salty meal and a long sweaty walk are different problems. This course will not tell you how much to drink. It will say: keeping water in range is work, and it trades with heat dumping when you sweat.

Fever sometimes shows up in first-year talk as “the body boiling the germs.” That is a cartoon, and this file will not become a pathogen story. A higher set point, when it happens, is a regulated change, not a motivational poster. We will not treat fever. We will not tell you when to worry. If you are sick, this HTML page is not your advisor.

You cannot maximize everything

A machine-fantasy of the body says every system should be at 100% all the time: max strength, max calm, max immunity, max focus. Living systems do not work that way. Resources are finite in a day. Blood going to working muscle is not going somewhere else in the same moment. Heat dumped at the skin is water spent. A first-year wellness sentence that wants every slider at the top is not biology. It is an ad.

“Boost immunity” is the plaque of this unit. Immune responses (kept conceptual, no pathogen work) are many processes, not a tank you fill with a product. A response can be costly: energy, tissue irritation, feeling wretched. That cost is one reason “boost” is a bad verb. You do not want every defense running at once any more than you want every building’s fire sprinklers on because fire exists. This is not medical advice about suppressing or stimulating anything. It is a refusal of the tank metaphor.

Subject facts stay conservative. We will not name drugs. We will not interpret your pulse. We will not give a target heart rate. We will not talk you through a supplement. The public habit is the same as Unit 1: parts, interaction, consequence — plus the new word, trade. What is kept? What is spent? What fails if the spend cannot be afforded in this room?

Practice will ask you to name the trade and to strip a boost. Homework is one trade from an ordinary day — heat on a walk, thirst after salt, pulse after stairs you already use — written as keep / spend / what would count against your sentence. If a sentence would only work as a diagnosis, tear it up.

Practice

Which sentence names a trade?

Pick the sentence that says what is kept and what is spent — without diagnosing anyone.

Practice

Strip the machine ad

Ad version: “Boost your immunity and optimize every system so your body can perform at 100%.”

Rewrite as one trade: a variable in a range, a spend, a condition where the spend buys less. No boost, no optimize, no 100%, no product, no diagnosis.

Homework

Turn this in if an instructor is running the course. If you are working alone, write it anyway. 200–300 words.

Pick one ordinary trade from a day you actually lived: sweating on a walk, thirst, or pulse after stairs you already climb (skip stairs if you should not use them). Write: what is being kept in range, what is spent or reallocated, and what would count against your sentence. Then write two sentences this unit refuses (a boost ad, a diagnosis). Do not give advice. Do not interpret a number as health. Do not discuss pathogens or treatments.
