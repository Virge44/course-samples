Module 5 · Responsible adoption · async · ~20 minutes

A note for Monday

One class. Two weeks. A six-line note a colleague can read while the copier jams. You do not need a committee for that.

3 objectives Six-line note 1 homework

You leave able to

  1. Tell a colleague, chair, or PD lead what you are trying, in plain language, and whether it is optional or required.
  2. Name what you will tell students this week.
  3. Sketch a two-week pilot you can actually run — or name why you will wait.

Lesson

Without a committee

You do not need a task force to try one verified draft or one activity card. You need a class you already teach, a rule you can say out loud, and one person who will know you are trying it. If your school has already named a tool and a policy, stay inside both. If it has not, stay inside Module 1’s red lines and Module 2’s talk-first habit. Do not wait for a rollout that is not coming this month.

There is no partner school in this module. There is you, your roster, and whoever sits down the hall.

Optional vs required

A first pilot should almost always be optional-with-disclosure, or teacher-side only (you use the tool; students do not). Required student use needs an offline path, an approved tool, and a reason that serves the learning goal. If you cannot name those three, do not require it.

Teacher-side use: you generate a discussion stem or an exit ticket, you verify, you own it. Students never have to open a chatbot. That is a complete week-one move.

Student-side use: they run one narrow step (three strategies, two counterarguments), they disclose, they still perform. That is a week-two move if devices and policy allow it.

What to tell students

Say it in the room, not only in a syllabus they will not reread. Four sentences are enough: what is allowed this week; what must be disclosed; what the tool may not write; that if you are unsure about authorship you will talk first. K–12: say it so a guardian could understand it in an email. Higher ed: say it so it matches the syllabus line you rewrote in Module 2.

Do not announce a new required tool on a Monday and grade with it on Wednesday. Do not announce a ban you cannot teach. Do not outsource the explanation to a detector warning.

Talking to a colleague, a chair, a PD lead

They do not need a framework deck. They need six lines they can read while the copier jams: what you are trying; which class; what is allowed and not; how you will check; what you need from them (often: nothing yet); when you will report back.

If you have no colleague to send it to, the note still works as an LMS journal. The point is to make the trial visible to one other adult — or to yourself in writing — so it does not evaporate after this file closes.

A two-week sketch

Week 1. Tell students the rule for this class. Use the tool once on your side, or not at all. Verify before anything they see. Keep the red lines.

Week 2. Run the Module 4 card, or run the unaided diagnostic you decided was the right stop rule. Send the six-line note. Keep one thing you will change after you see the student work.

If two weeks is too much — no approved tool, a high-stakes window, a class that needs a clean baseline — write that down. Waiting with a reason is adoption. Waiting for a committee that has not been named is not.
