Classroom Activities That Hold

Build one activity card you could run on Thursday. Narrow role, a stop rule, and a path that still works if three kids have no device.

3 objectives Activity card 1 homework

You leave able to

  1. Assign AI a narrow role that serves a learning goal — or name when AI stays out.
  2. Build an activity card with timing, student steps, disclosure, success evidence, and a stop rule.
  3. Stress-test the card for equity and integrity (offline path; human performance still graded).

Lesson

Narrow the role

Strong designs give AI one job. Weak designs let it do the assignment. If the goal is that students improve argument structure, the model may propose three revision strategies. It may not write the paragraph they submit. If the goal is that they evaluate a counterargument, the model may produce two opposing claims. The graded work is the critique.

If you cannot say in one line what the tool is not allowed to do, you do not have a role. You have a hope.

When not to use AI

Skip AI when
You need clean evidence of unaided skill — a fluency sample, a cold write, an oral defense only.
Skip AI when
The task requires private data, secure items, or emotionally sensitive disclosure.
Skip AI when
Students lack access equity and the activity would widen gaps without an offline path.
Use AI when
You want more options to critique, faster first drafts of non-graded materials you will edit, or differentiated entry points.

A timed in-class diagnostic stays analog because you need a baseline, not because “AI is always unethical.” Detectors are not a design strategy.

The card

Write it so a substitute — or a tired you, on Thursday — could run it. Title. Time box. One-line AI role. Numbered student steps. A disclosure line students can actually write. What you will collect as success evidence. A stop rule: when AI leaves the room.

K–12: standards, pacing, guardians, mixed devices. Higher ed: outcomes, adult learners, syllabus alignment. Same card. Different constraints in the Context line.

Stress test before you teach it

Four mitigations, one trap. Narrow role. Disclosure students can write. Human performance still required for the grade. An offline or low-access path. Do not make detectors the integrity plan. If three students have no device, the card still has to work. If two students finish the AI step in a minute, the card still has a next move.

Homework

Pilot that card in one class this term, or write why you will not. “Why not” is a complete answer — no device path, no approved tool, the goal needs a clean unaided sample. Do not invent a public showcase.

Homework: Name the class. Paste the activity card, or three sentences on why you will not run it this term. If you run it: what you will collect as evidence, and one thing you will change after the first try.
