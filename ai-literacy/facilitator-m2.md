# Facilitator note — Module 2: Integrity Without Panic

For a PD lead running this async. Colleague voice.

**Author frame (if asked).** Higher-ed faculty / faculty developer; not a K–12 classroom teacher; not a software engineer. K–12 cases are for the audience.

## Time

About 25 minutes: lesson (~10), practice (~10), homework (~5). Practice is in `module-2.html`: place three uses on the spectrum, rewrite one policy sentence, one borderline case. Process conference first.

## Objectives

1. Place common AI uses on an integrity spectrum (prohibited / disclose / required tool / redesign).
2. Separate authorship, privacy, bias, and labor so every issue is not “cheating.”
3. Rewrite foggy student-facing policy into language that names allowed, disclose, and what happens next.

## Homework

They rewrite *their* syllabus or classroom AI line in plain language. Collect in an LMS journal if you have one. You are looking for allowed / disclose / not allowed / talk-first — not legal polish.

## Common snags

- Wanting a detector policy. Detectors are weak evidence alone. This module teaches process, not software.
- “Any AI touch is cheating.” Hold the spectrum. Conditional use with disclosure is a real design choice.
- Rewriting into longer fog. Push for sentences a student can act on this week.
- Treating a fabricated quote as a cheating case. That is accuracy / bias in materials you assign.
- Jumping to office referral on the borderline case. Conference first.

## What not to claim

Do not invent a campus or district partner. Do not present the sample policy as legal advice. Local policy still governs.

## Optional 15-minute debrief

One person reads their rewrite. The room checks: allowed, disclose, not allowed, privacy, talk-first. Stop.
