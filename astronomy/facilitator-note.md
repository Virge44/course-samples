# Facilitator note — Astronomy 101

Draft sample. Not official NC A&T curriculum. Do not apply.

## What not to claim

He did not teach astronomy as a content expert. Lecturer courses were Critical Writing, Humanities, and Analytical Reasoning. As Director of Online Learning (2008–2012) he designed an online course called *Scientific Development & Social Change* — design/ops, not an astronomy appointment. Do not invent labs, observatories, credentials, or “he taught this at A&T.” Do not introduce him as an astronomer. Do not treat *Scientific Development & Social Change* as an astronomy course he taught.

## How to run it

Eight units plus the home. About 4–6 hours if they do the practice. Open `index.html` in a browser. No telescope is required. Homework is journal- or paper-sized. This file does not collect it.

The public habit: say what the light allows, name the delay or the geometry, stop before the story.

## The eight (do not skip 1–2)

1. Scale and light delay
2. Sky as clock — rotation, tilt/seasons, Moon phase (not Earth’s shadow)
3. Gravity and orbits — falling sideways
4. Light as data
5. Solar system as leftovers
6. Stars — fuel, mass/lifetime (Sun will not supernova)
7. Galaxies — nested address
8. Observing log — naked-eye or a **public** image

## Common snags

Brighter means closer. Seasons from “Earth closer to the Sun in summer.” Moon phases as Earth’s shadow. Astronauts float because there is no gravity. A planet’s color treated like a star’s color. The Sun will explode as a supernova. Inventing a telescope session they did not have — send them to a naked-eye sky or a labeled public image. Astrology or classified/military space systems — out of scope.

If someone asks whether he taught astronomy at A&T: no.
