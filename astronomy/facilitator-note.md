## What “good” looks like

The public habit: say what the light allows, name the delay or the geometry, stop before the story. Fluency is not the grade. A claim a classmate can check is the grade. Homework is journal- or paper-sized. This file does not collect it. No telescope is required.

## Sequence (do not skip 1–2)

1. Scale and light delay — what one night can and cannot support
2. Sky as clock — rotation, tilt/seasons, Moon phase (not Earth’s shadow)
3. Gravity and orbits — falling sideways; “zero gravity” is the wrong phrase
4. Light as data — color, spectrum, what a color can tell you
5. Solar system as leftovers — disk, not a designed family
6. Stars — fuel, mass/lifetime, death as a process (Sun will not supernova)
7. Galaxies — nested address; we are not the center of the Milky Way
8. Observing log — naked-eye or a **public** image; observation vs inference

## Common snags

- Brighter means closer. That is Unit 1.
- Seasons from “Earth closer to the Sun in summer.” Perihelion is in January. Unit 2.
- Moon phases as Earth’s shadow. That is an eclipse, not a phase. Unit 2.
- Astronauts float because there is no gravity. They fall with the station. Unit 3.
- A planet’s color treated like a star’s color. Reflected light vs its own glow. Unit 4.
- The solar system as a planned set. Leftover disk. Unit 5.
- The Sun will explode as a supernova. It will not. Unit 6.
- Inventing a telescope session they did not have. Send them to a naked-eye sky or a labeled public image. Unit 8.
- Astrology, horoscopes, or classified/military space systems. Out of scope. Refuse the costume.
