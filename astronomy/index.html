Who this is for

First-year and gen-ed college students, mixed preparation. Adult tone. You do not need a telescope, a night-sky app, or a physics course behind you. You need a habit: say what the light allows you to claim, and stop before the story outruns the evidence.

This is not a catalog astronomy course. It is not a lab sequence. It is a compressed 101: eight units plus this syllabus page. Astrology is out of scope. Classified or military space systems are out of scope. Public-domain, textbook-level facts only.

What the course asks you to do

Astronomy, in this sample, is a public habit of careful claim. A bright point is not yet a distance. A color is not yet a temperature unless you say what kind of object you are looking at. Each unit has three measurable objectives, a lesson, clickable practice with feedback, and one homework you could turn in. If you do the practices, plan on about four to six hours for the whole sample. A live semester would slow this down, add a textbook, and give you more sky time. This file is the spine, not the full term.

Light takes time. The sky you see is a mix of then. Scale and delay come before romance.

Sequence

UnitTimeYou leave able to
1. What you can claim from a night sky
Scale, light delay		 ~35 min Separate a checkable claim from a story, and name a light-travel time.
2. The sky as a clock
Rotation, seasons, Moon		 ~40 min Explain day, season, and Moon phase as geometry, not mood.
3. Gravity and orbits
Plain language		 ~40 min Say why something can fall forever sideways, and why “zero gravity” is the wrong phrase.
4. Light as data
Spectrum, color		 ~40 min Say what a color can and cannot tell you about a star or a planet.
5. The solar system as leftovers
A family, not a design		 ~40 min Place worlds as leftover disk material, not as a planned set.
6. Stars: fuel, lifetime, death
Death as a process		 ~40 min Connect mass to lifetime, and name the Sun’s path without a supernova.
7. Galaxies and “where are we”
A nested address		 ~35 min Give a conservative address: planet, star, disk, galaxy, group.
8. A short observing log
Naked-eye or a public image		 ~40 min Write a log that splits what you saw from what you infer. No telescope required.

How work is judged (if you have a section)

If an instructor used this sample in a live class, the standard would be public, not clever. Did you name a delay? Did you refuse a brighter-means-closer shortcut? Did you split phase from eclipse? Did you leave a log a classmate could check? Fluency is not the grade. The conservative claim is the grade.

Homework lives in your own words. Paste the prompt into a journal, a discussion board, or a printed page. This file does not collect it.
