Who this is for

First-year and gen-ed college students, mixed preparation, adult tone. You do not need a lab coat or a chemistry job. You need a habit: name the sample, say whether its identity survived, leave a check a classmate could run on a sentence — not on a bench.

This is a course-structure sample. It is written to show that an educator / online-learning lead can sequence a 101: objectives, a readable lesson, practice with feedback, one homework. It is not written from a chemistry appointment. It is not “I taught this at A&T.”

What the course asks you to do

Chemistry here is public bookkeeping about matter. A private hunch is not yet a claim. Each unit has three measurable objectives, a 600–900 word lesson, clickable practice with feedback, and one homework you could turn in. If you do the practices, plan on about four to six hours for the whole sample. A live semester would slow this down, add a supervised lab taught by a chemist, and give you more drafts. This file is the compressed 101: the spine, not the full term, and not a lab manual.

A chemical claim is a sentence about what something is made of, or whether it turned into something else, that a careful person could check against evidence. If you cannot name the substance and the check, you do not yet have chemistry. You have a slogan.

Sequence

UnitTimeYou leave able to
1. What a chemical claim is
Substance, change, evidence		 ~35 min Split a sentence into sample, identity, and a check that is not a lab recipe.
2. Atoms and the periodic table
The table as a tool		 ~40 min Use atomic number, groups, and a few symbols the way a table is meant to be used: looked up, not memorized as a personality test.
3. Bonding
Why things stick		 ~40 min Say in plain language why a salt crystal and a water molecule stay together, without a “how to make.”
4. Moles and counting
Without magic		 ~40 min Treat the mole as a counting word, and convert grams to moles with a printed molar mass.
5. Reactions as accounts
What is conserved		 ~40 min Read an equation as books: atoms conserved; coefficients change, subscripts do not.
6. Acids, bases, and pH
Food and cleaning — conceptual		 ~40 min Place a typical pH number on a scale and refuse mixing instructions for household products.
7. Energy of reactions
Felt experience + a number		 ~35 min Match endothermic / exothermic to a felt change and a signed ΔH, using commercial products as claims, not recipes.
8. Reading a label
A short evidence writeup		 ~40 min Turn an ingredient list into a short evidence paragraph. Not a synthesis.

How work is judged (if you have a section)

If an instructor used this sample in a live class, the standard would be public, not clever. Did you name a substance? Did you say whether identity survived? Did you leave a check that is a sentence, a number from a label, or a count of atoms on a page — not a procedure? Fluency is not the grade. The checkable step is the grade.

Homework lives in your own words. Paste the prompt into a journal, a discussion board, or a printed page. This file does not collect it. Do not photograph a lab you did not have permission to enter. Do not mix products to “see what happens.”
