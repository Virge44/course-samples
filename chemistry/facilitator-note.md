# Facilitator note — Chemistry 101

Draft sample. Not official NC A&T curriculum. Do not apply. Do not run it as a lab.

## What not to claim

He did not teach chemistry as a content expert. Lecturer courses were Critical Writing, Humanities, and Analytical Reasoning (NC A&T, 2004–2016). As Director of Online Learning (2008–2012) he designed an online course called Scientific Development & Social Change — design/ops, not a chemistry appointment. Do not invent labs, credentials, or “he taught this at A&T.” Do not introduce him as a chemist or a lab instructor.

## Safety (non-negotiable)

No lab recipes, mixing instructions, extraction how-tos, or “how to make” steps. No explosives, no drug synthesis, no weaponization, no chlorine gas. Household products are labels and typical published values only. If a student asks how to combine vinegar and a cleaner, refuse. If they ask for a homemade cold pack, send them back to a commercial package claim.

## How to run it

Eight units plus the home. About 4–6 hours if they do the practice. Double-click `index.html`. Do not send people to a Drive preview. Homework is journal- or paper-sized. This file does not collect it.

## Sequence (do not skip 1)

1. Chemical claim — substance, change, evidence
2. Atoms and the periodic table as a tool
3. Bonding in plain language (no synthesis)
4. Moles as a counting word
5. Equations as accounts
6. pH conceptual (food / cleaning labels; do not mix)
7. Energy: felt + signed ΔH (commercial packs as claims)
8. Label → short evidence writeup

## Common snags

“There was a reaction” with no substance named. Personality paragraphs for elements. Turning Unit 3 or 5 into a how-to. Mixing household products for Unit 6. Cutting open a cold pack for Unit 7. Unit 8 becoming a recipe.

If someone asks whether he taught chemistry at A&T: no.
