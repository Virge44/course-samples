## Time

Eight units plus the syllabus home. About 4–6 hours if students do the practices; a live semester would stretch this with readings and a supervised lab taught by a chemist. Each unit: lesson (~12–15 min), in-module practice with feedback (~15 min), homework prompt they could turn in (~5 min to copy; writing happens outside the file). Practice lives in `unit-1.html` … `unit-8.html`. Double-click `index.html`. Do not send people to a Drive preview.

## Safety (non-negotiable)

This sample contains **no** lab recipes, mixing instructions, extraction how-tos, or “how to make” steps. No explosives, no drug synthesis, no weaponization, no chlorine gas. Household products are labels and typical published values only. If a student asks how to combine vinegar and a cleaner, refuse. If they ask for a homemade cold pack or hand warmer, send them back to a commercial package claim. Thought experiments and reading labels only.

## What “good” looks like

The public habit: name the sample, say whether identity survived, leave a check a classmate can run on a sentence, a table square, an atom tally, or a printed panel. Fluency is not the grade. Homework is journal- or paper-sized. This file does not collect it.

## Sequence (do not skip 1)

1. Chemical claim — substance, change, evidence
2. Atoms and the periodic table as a tool
3. Bonding in plain language (no synthesis)
4. Moles as a counting word
5. Equations as accounts — what is conserved
6. pH conceptual (food / cleaning labels; do not mix)
7. Energy: felt + signed ΔH (commercial packs as claims)
8. Label → short evidence writeup (not a synthesis)

## Common snags

- Treating Unit 1 as a warmup and writing “there was a reaction” with no substance named.
- Personality paragraphs for elements (“oxygen is social”). The table is a lookup tool.
- Turning Unit 3 or 5 into a how-to. Coefficients are books, not a kit.
- Mixing household products for Unit 6 “to see the pH.” Stop the section if that starts.
- Cutting open a cold pack for Unit 7. The package claim is the source.
- Unit 8 becoming a recipe or a reverse-engineering project. Five audit sentences, then stop.
- Introducing the author as a chemist or as having taught chemistry at A&T. He did not.

## What not to claim

Do not introduce the author as a software engineer, a chemist, or a lab instructor. Do not call this official NC A&T curriculum or a current offering. Do not invent school partners or student data. Do not use hype language if you add a welcome note. Keep the sample banner.

## Optional 20-minute live debrief

Only if a section exists. One Unit 1 split (sample / claim / check). One Unit 6 warning line read aloud. One Unit 8 sentence a classmate could refuse by pointing at the same label. Stop. Do not turn it into a demo fair.
