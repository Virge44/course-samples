Who it is for

First-year college students, mixed preparation, often underprepared for the kind of writing college actually grades. You do not need to love writing. You need to put a claim on the table that a reader who did not have to show up can test, refuse, or use.

If you already write fluent five-paragraph themes, this course will still slow you down. Fluency is not the same as a claim. If you freeze at a blank page, start with Unit 6 after you finish Unit 3. The sequence is the course; the units are short enough to finish tired.

What writing is, here

Writing is a public act. A paper is not a diary with MLA formatting. A discussion post is not a feeling. You are responsible for a claim, for reasons a skeptic can inspect, and for the reader you named. Voice will take care of itself if the sentences have a job.

A claim someone else can test. If nobody could disagree, you filled a page.

Course outcomes

By the end of the eight units, you should be able to:

  1. Name a specific reader, a purpose you can watch, and a local stake for a piece of college writing.
  2. Read another writer for the move they are making, not only for the topic.
  3. Write a thesis that can be wrong — and change it when a counterargument requires it.
  4. Separate reasons and evidence from restatement and from “I feel.”
  5. Build paragraphs that each do one job, produce a full messy draft, and revise the argument rather than only the commas.
  6. Use a source as a voice in your argument without disappearing as the writer.

Sequence

Semester-shaped, compressed. Do the units in order. Each one is a lesson you can finish in one sitting, a clickable practice with feedback, and one homework you could actually turn in. Total student time if you do the practices: about four to six hours. This is not fifteen weeks of reading dumps.

How the units work

Each unit has three measurable objectives, lesson prose a tired first-year can finish, clickable practice that talks back, and one homework of 150–400 words (or a revision of your own paragraph). Practice is not a quiz for a score. It is a move you will need on the homework.

If an instructor is running this async or hybrid, turn homework in where they say — an LMS journal, a shared folder, paper. If you are working alone, write it anyway. The course does not store it.
