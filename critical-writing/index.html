Critical Writing 101

The reader did not have to show up. They have a prior view and other things to do. If they could not disagree, you filled a page.

Self-paced No login No server First-year writing

I co-created and taught Critical Writing in University Studies at NC A&T. This file is that course compressed: a claim someone else can test, reasons they can inspect, a revision when the evidence turns.

You name a reader. You put a position on the table they could refuse. You give them something to push on besides your mood. Voice takes care of itself if the sentences have a job.

Paragraphs in the units are invented for practice. They are not student records.

If you finish the eight units

  1. Name a reader, a change you can watch, and a local cost if they shrug.
  2. Read another writer for the move they are making, not only the topic.
  3. Write a thesis a classmate could refuse — and change it when an objection requires it.
  4. Separate a reason from a restatement and from “I feel.”
  5. Build paragraphs that each do one job, get a full messy draft, and revise the argument before the commas.
  6. Use a source as a voice in your argument without disappearing as the writer.

Sequence

Do the units in order. Each one is a sitting: a lesson, a practice in the page, and one short piece you could turn in. Four to six hours if you do the practices. Not fifteen weeks of reading dumps.

UnitTimeWhat you can do
1. Who you’re writing to
Audience, purpose, stakes		 ~35 min Name a reader with a prior belief, a purpose you can watch, and a cost if they stay unconvinced.
2. Reading as a writer
What the other person is doing		 ~35 min Mark a writer’s claim and name the move of a sentence — not just the topic.
3. A claim that can be wrong
Thesis that can change		 ~40 min Rewrite a dead thesis so a classmate could disagree, and name one objection.
4. Reasons and evidence
Not “I feel”		 ~40 min Rank evidence by how well it tests a claim, and separate a reason from a restatement.
5. Paragraphs that do a job
One move each		 ~35 min Label the job of a paragraph and rewrite a topic sentence that only labels a subject.
6. Drafting without performing
Get a bad full draft		 ~40 min Produce a complete messy draft with a claim, reasons, and an ending — not a polished fragment.
7. Revision is not editing
Re-see the argument		 ~40 min Change the claim or the structure before you chase commas, and cut what does not serve the argument.
8. Using sources without disappearing
Plus a short portfolio close		 ~45 min Keep your claim visible when a source enters, and close with a short argument you would stand behind.
