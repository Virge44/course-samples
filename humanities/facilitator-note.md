# Facilitator note — Humanities 101 (draft sample)

For someone who might run this async or hybrid. One page. Not official NC A&T curriculum. Not a live institutional course.

Director of Online Learning, 2008–2012. MFA, Creative Writing, UNCG. Published poet. This sample is written in the grain of those Humanities courses. It is not those courses. ## How to run it

Keep the folder together. Point students at `index.html` in a desktop browser, not a Drive preview. Practices are in-page JavaScript. Collect homework in your LMS, a shared folder, or on paper.

Eight units, in order. About four to six hours if they do the practices. Compressed 101, not an anthology slog. If you stretch it across a term, use the units as weekly spines and add *your* assigned public-domain or licensed readings. Do not add copyrighted criticism or recent lyrics to these files. Do not treat the Elpenor retelling or the poem *Receipt* as published sources in a bibliography.

## What to collect

| Unit | Turn-in | What to skim for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Two rewritten questions + one paragraph | A made thing, a doing verb, a living person. Flag “what happened,” “theme,” “I feel,” “human condition.” |
| 2 | Marked line + 150–200 words on what it is doing | Stayed with one passage. Doing, not “about.” |
| 3 | Context note they would keep, one they would cut, why | The kept note helps you hear a sentence. The cut one is a chapter in costume. |
| 4 | Claim that can be wrong + one honest refusal | Not a theme-card. A classmate could start “No, because…” |
| 5 | Two voices in pressure, 200–250 words | Conversation, not a quote stack. Each voice does a job. |
| 6 | Who is left out + rewritten heroic sentence | A named someone, not “society.” Cost visible. |
| 7 | Interpretive essay, 500–700 words | Claim, stayed-with evidence, one other voice, a local stake. No biography dump opening. |
| 8 | One work, one claim, one public sentence | The public sentence could be said off campus without a theme-card. |

Grade a claim a stranger could refuse by pointing at the same lines. Not fluency. Not “appreciation.”

## Common snags

- Treating Unit 1 as a warmup and writing theme papers anyway. The question type *is* the course.
- Surveying a whole book in Unit 2. One passage. If they cannot point at a line, they have not looked.
- Unit 3 becoming a history mini-lecture. Context is a hearing aid. If they never get back to the sentence, they drowned.
- Unit 5 as a quote farm. Two voices in pressure beat six voices in agreement.
- Unit 7 opening with the author’s biography. Biography is context, not a claim.
- Students asking if this *is* the old A&T Humanities sequence. Say: written in that grain; not official; not current.

## What not to claim

Do not introduce me as a software engineer. Do not call this official NC A&T curriculum or a live A&T section. Do not invent school partners or student data. Do not treat the Elpenor retelling or *Receipt* as published translations or as student work. They are teaching objects.

## Optional live 20 minutes

Only if a cohort exists. Three volunteers read their Unit 1 rewritten question. Class says whether it names a doing and a living person. One Unit 4 theme-card rewritten on the board. Stop.

## Accessibility

Pages are keyboardable. Practices give feedback in-page, not only as color. If you need a no-JavaScript path, the homework prompts still work; the in-page practice will not.
