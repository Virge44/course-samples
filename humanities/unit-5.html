Unit 5 · Conversation, not a dump · ~35 minutes

Other voices

A second text is not a second quote. It is pressure. If both voices “support” you, you stacked souvenirs.

3 objectives Pair + pressure 1 homework

When you finish

  1. Tell a conversation (two voices that pressure each other) from a quote dump (several voices that all agree with you).
  2. Name the job of a second voice: it answers, limits, or complicates the first — it does not decorate.
  3. Write two or three sentences in which Douglass, Dunbar, Equiano, Elpenor, or Receipt does a job to the other, not a chorus.

Lesson

Two is a conversation; six is a parade

First-year papers love company. Three quotations, all saying roughly the same thing the writer already believed. The page looks researched. It is a dump. If five dead people agree with you, you do not have to risk a claim. One made thing answers another, and you are responsible for saying what the answer does.

A second voice earns its keep by changing what you can say about the first. It can answer: Dunbar’s “we” after Douglass’s “I.” It can limit: Equiano’s look at the ship after a heroic reading of voyage. It can complicate: Elpenor’s oar after a claim that only named heroes get monuments. If the second voice does none of those, cut it.

A third voice, kept short

Olaudah Equiano, The Interesting Narrative of the Life of Olaudah Equiano (1789). Public domain. He is looking, for the first time, at the ship that will take him:

Public-domain excerpt · Equiano 1789

When I looked round the ship too, and saw a large furnace or copper boiling, and a multitude of black people of every description chained together, every one of their countenances expressing dejection and sorrow, I no longer doubted of my fate; and, quite overpowered with horror and anguish, I fell motionless on the deck and fainted.

Olaudah Equiano, The Interesting Narrative (London, 1789), ch. 2. Short look. Not a chapter. Not a scholarly edition.

Enough context to hear it (Unit 3): this is a Middle Passage look, written later in English for a British public Equiano means to move. Too much: a full biography, every debate about his birthplace. Return to the sentence. The doing is in the look that ends doubt — “I no longer doubted of my fate” — and in the body that drops. Knowledge here is not a ladder. It is a confirmation that collapses him.

Pressure, not a theme-match

A dump version of Douglass + Equiano: “Both writers show the horrors of slavery and the importance of freedom.” True, slack, finished before you started. A conversation: Equiano’s look makes knowledge a faint; Douglass’s three sentences make knowledge a curse that leaves him standing, eyes open, with no ladder. Same word-family — seeing — two different spends.

Douglass + Dunbar, dump: “Both men write about the Black experience and hiding pain.” Conversation: Douglass’s “I” claims a pit in the first person and publishes it. Dunbar’s “we” claims a grin that is a debt, and the mouth that pays it. One voice makes disclosure the work. The other makes paid speech the work. If you let them chorus, you lose both doings.

Elpenor + Equiano is a stranger pairing, which is useful. Both look at a vessel and a crowd of bodies. Do not force them into “voyages are hard.” The pressure is about who gets to be the one who looks and then speaks. Odysseus tells Elpenor’s demand in the hero’s mouth. Equiano tells his own faint.

Receipt + Dunbar: a boy mouths a verse he is not supposed to know; Dunbar’s “we” mouths subtleties as payment. Dump: “both are about hiding.” Conversation: one mouth is unsounded and out of hour; the other is public, grinning, in debt. Silence and paid speech are not the same mask.

How to put them on a page

One claim (Unit 4). Then voice A, one short quote, what it is doing. Then voice B, one short quote, the job it does to A — answers, limits, complicates. Then one sentence of yours that could not have been written with only one of them. If that last sentence works with either text deleted, you dumped. Stay under two quotations each. If you need a third writer, you are probably scared of the claim.

Do not introduce a voice with a biography paragraph. Name, year, one hearing-aid fact if the sentence requires it, then the line. The living person you are writing for can hear a look, a curse, a grin, an oar. Let the second voice change the first. That is the whole unit.

Practice

Which pairing is a conversation?

Pick the account that lets the second voice change the first. The others are dumps in different clothes.

Practice

What is the second voice doing?

First voice: Douglass names literacy as a view of the pit with no ladder. Second voice: Equiano’s look at the ship ends doubt and drops him to the deck. What job is Equiano doing to Douglass?

Homework

200–250 words.

Put two course texts in pressure. Quote each once, short. Name the job of the second voice: it answers, limits, or complicates the first. End with one sentence you could not have written with only one text. If both quotations “support” you, rewrite until the second one costs you something. Do not add a third writer. Do not open with two biographies.
