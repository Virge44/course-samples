Unit 8 · They pick a real place · ~40 minutes

A local-place writeup

A hill, a river, or a weather week you can point to. Three Earth claims a classmate could check. One hole in the record. No invented field trip.

3 objectives Practice with feedback 1 homework

What you'll do

In this sitting:

  1. Choose a real hill, river reach, or weather week you can point to (a stand-there sentence, a USGS or NWS page, a dated observation).
  2. Write three Earth claims about it a classmate could check — and refuse invented fossils, partners, and quotes.
  3. Name one limit of the record: what you could not see or did not look up.

Lesson

This is the last unit of a compressed 101. A live semester would give you a textbook, more maps, and more drafts. The spine is the same: an Earth claim a classmate can check, plus a hole in the record. The close is not a performance and not a field trip this folder invented. You pick a real hill, a real river reach, or a real weather week you can point to. Real means a place you have stood, or a public page with a name and a date — a USGS topographic map, an NWS climate week, a gauge, a roadcut you can describe without fiction. If you cannot point to it, you do not have a place yet. You have a mood.

Do not invent a partner campus hike. Do not invent a school van. Do not write “our class walked the ravine with Dr. —” unless that actually happened in a section that exists without this file. This sample will not supply a headline hill. Greensboro, or wherever you are, has ordinary relief: a creek, a slope, a week of highs and lows. Ordinary is enough. Extraordinary claims need extraordinary checks.

Three Earth claims is the load. Not thirty. Not a research paper. Claim one can be materials or form: what the hill is made of, or how steep it is, or which way the creek runs, tied to a map or an outcrop. Claim two can be process: a river doing work, a slope shedding water, a front that moved through between Monday and Thursday. Claim three can be a limit: what you could not see — the water table, the age of the rock, the next basin, the climate window behind one week of weather. That third claim is Unit 1 finished in a place. Incomplete is not a confession of laziness. It is the working condition.

Use the earlier units as tools, not as labels to sprinkle. If you name sandstone, give a formation reason (Unit 2). If you mention a ridge of earthquakes two states away, do not drag it in to sound like a major; only use plates if the place actually sits on a story you can check (Unit 3). Water and weather will do more work for most students than a forced subduction sentence. Climate belongs if you have a normal or a multi-year record for that place, not if you want a sermon on a Tuesday afternoon (Units 5–6). A gravel path or a brick is a resource claim if you actually trace it (Unit 7).

Refuse extra furniture. Do not add a flood of record you did not look up. Do not add a quarry owner’s quote you did not hear. Do not add a fossil that would be nice. An honest hole — “I do not have a geologic map for this block; the county GIS would be the next check” — is a better close than a polished fiction. Adult tone. You may like the place. Liking is allowed. Purple nature writing is not a check.

Say the claim. Show the step. Let someone else mark it. If you can do that on a real hill, creek, or weather week, you have finished the sample.

Practice here is a gate. You will mark whether a place is pointable, refuse a fake outcrop, and draft three labeled claims. Homework is the writeup itself — the thing you could turn in.

Practice

Draft three labeled claims

Use a real place you can point to — or, for this practice only, this public-style kit: a named creek at a street you can stand on; a USGS contour; a week of highs and lows from a public NWS page. Three short labeled lines: materials or form; process; limit. No fake quarry.

Homework

A page you could hand in. Not a field trip.

Write a 300–450 word local-place close. At the top, give the pointer: place name and street or lat-long; or USGS map name; or NWS/climate page and dates. Then three labeled Earth claims (form or materials; process; limit). Rules: do not invent a field trip, a partner school, a fossil, or a quote. If a map or a date is missing, say so. Adult tone. A classmate who was not there should still be able to check your steps. This is the course close, not a nature essay.
