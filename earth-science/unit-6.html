Unit 6 · Evidence, not a rant · ~40 minutes

Climate as a measured argument

Variable, window, method, uncertainty. Not a sermon. Not a hoax. A classmate should be able to check the series.

3 objectives Practice with feedback 1 homework

Learning objectives

By the end of this unit you will be able to:

  1. Define climate as a measured pattern over decades, not a mood and not a single storm.
  2. Name two kinds of evidence used in climate claims (instrument record; a proxy such as ice cores or tree rings) and one limit of each.
  3. Separate a measurement from a policy slogan — and refuse both a rant and a conspiracy.

Lesson

Climate is a measured argument. It is not a rant, and it is not a conspiracy. A climate claim says: over this stretch of years, in this place or for the globe, this variable (temperature, precipitation, ice, sea level, a gas) had this pattern, measured by this method, with this uncertainty. If you cannot name the variable, the window, and the method, you do not yet have a climate claim. You have a mood. Unit 5 already split weather from climate. This unit asks you to treat the long pattern as evidence a classmate could check, including the parts that are incomplete.

The instrument record is the first line. Thermometers, rain gauges, and, later, satellites and buoys. A global temperature series is not one backyard thermometer. It is many stations, processed, with known problems (station moves, urban heat, coverage gaps) that researchers try to correct and that skeptics are allowed to ask about. Asking “how was this homogenized?” is an evidence habit. Saying “they faked the thermometers, case closed” is a rant in the other direction. This sample will not walk you through a full reanalysis. It will insist that a number without a method is not yet a check.

Proxies extend the story past the instrument era. Tree rings, ice cores, corals, lake and ocean sediments, boreholes — each records something related to climate, with a calibration and a limit. Ice cores trap old air; they are a main reason we can speak about carbon dioxide before 1958. They are not a time machine with no error bar. A tree ring can track moisture or temperature depending on the site. A proxy is a stand-in. Unit 1’s incomplete record applies: you do not have a weather station in the year 1400. You have a substitute with a stated weakness. Name the weakness or you are preaching.

Carbon dioxide is a greenhouse gas. That is basic physics taught long before the modern culture fight: some gases absorb outgoing infrared radiation and change the energy budget. Water vapor is also a greenhouse gas; it responds quickly to temperature. CO2 is well mixed and has a measured rise. The Keeling record from Mauna Loa begins in 1958. Ice cores extend CO2 through earlier centuries and glacial cycles. The modern rise is large and fast compared with those ice-core swings. Those are conservative textbook sentences. They are not a party platform.

The Earth has warmed since the late nineteenth century in the instrument record. Multiple groups produce similar global curves. Attribution — how much of the recent warming is from human-emitted greenhouse gases versus other forcings (sun, volcanoes, internal variability) — is a scientific argument with a literature, not a vibe. 101 can say: greenhouse physics plus the measured CO2 rise plus the observed warming is the core package; other forcings are studied and do not currently replace that package. 101 should also say: a single snowstorm does not refute a trend, and a single heat wave does not, by itself, prove one. Trends need windows.

Policy is a different kind of claim. “What the temperature did” is not the same sentence as “what a legislature should do.” You may care about policy. This unit grades the measured argument: what was measured, over what time, by what method, what would change your mind. Uncertainty is real. Uncertainty is not the same as “we know nothing” and not the same as “the debate is only political.” If a source will not tell you the window or the method, it is not helping you, whatever flag it flies.

The author did not teach climate science as a content expert. He taught students to split a claim from a because. That is the whole point of putting this unit in an ID sample. Practice will ask you to mark a measured sentence, reject a rant and a conspiracy, and write one climate claim with a method and a hole.

Rant
The planet is doomed and anyone who asks a method question is evil.
Conspiracy
Every thermometer and ice core is a hoax, so no measurement counts.
Measured argument
Mauna Loa CO2 has risen since 1958; ice cores show a lower pre-industrial range. Method named. Window named.
Still a hole
One station is not the globe. A proxy has a calibration. Say so.

Practice

Write a climate claim with a method

One or two sentences: variable, time window, method (instruments, ice core, tree ring, satellite). Then one clause that names a limit. No sermon. No hoax plot.

Homework

You could turn this in on paper or paste it into a journal. This file does not collect it.

Write 180–250 words as a measured argument, not a speech. Pick one public series you can point to (a national climate page, Mauna Loa CO2, a published global temperature graph, a local climate normal). State: variable, window, method. State one uncertainty or gap. State one thing that would change your mind. Do not write a manifesto. Do not write a hoax plot. Do not invent a lab you sat in.
