## Time

Eight units plus the syllabus home. About 4–6 hours if students do the practices; a live semester would stretch this with a textbook, maps, and more drafts. Each unit: lesson (~12–18 min), in-module practice with feedback (~15 min), homework prompt they could turn in (~5 min to copy; writing happens outside the file). Practice lives in `unit-1.html` … `unit-8.html`. Double-click `index.html`. Do not send people to a Drive preview.

## What “good” looks like

The public habit: an Earth claim a classmate can check, plus an admitted gap in the record. Fluency is not the grade. A sentence with a scale, a rock type, a boundary, a gauge, a date, or a named method is the grade. Homework is journal- or paper-sized. This file does not collect it.

## Sequence (do not skip 1–3)

1. What an Earth claim is — deep time, incomplete record
2. Minerals and rocks as evidence — not decoration
3. Plate tectonics — the ground is not finished
4. Water — rivers, groundwater; “important” is not a claim
5. Atmosphere and weather — not a forecast app
6. Climate as a measured argument — evidence, not a rant and not a conspiracy
7. Earth resources and waste — stocks and flows
8. Close on a **real** hill, river, or weather week they can point to. No invented field trip.

## Common snags

- Treating “nature is amazing” as an Earth claim. That is Unit 1.
- Using *always* / *recently* with no scale. Deep time. Unit 1.
- Calling a rock “pretty” and stopping. Unit 2 wants a formation story.
- Treating the map as scenery. Mountains and quakes are process. Unit 3.
- “Water is important” with no path, no table, no floodplain. Unit 4.
- Reading an app icon as an explanation. Unit 5.
- Turning Unit 6 into a sermon or a conspiracy hunt. Send them back to what was measured, over what time, by what method, and what would change their mind.
- Inventing a mine, a landfill, or a local partner. Unit 7 is about a real object they already own.
- Inventing a field trip or a hill they have not seen. Unit 8: a place they can point to, or a public weather week with dates.
