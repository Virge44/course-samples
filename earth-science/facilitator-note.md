# Facilitator note — Earth Science 101

Draft sample. Not official NC A&T curriculum. Do not apply.

## What not to claim

He did not teach earth science as a content expert. Lecturer courses were Critical Writing, Humanities, and Analytical Reasoning (NC A&T, 2004–2016). As Director of Online Learning (2008–2012) he designed an online course called *Scientific Development & Social Change* — design/ops, not an earth-science appointment. Do not invent labs, field trips, credentials, or “I taught this at A&T.” Do not introduce him as a geologist. Subject facts are conservative textbook 101.

## How to run it

Eight units plus the home. About 4–6 hours if they do the practice. Open `index.html` in a browser, not a Drive preview. Homework is journal- or paper-sized. This file does not collect it.

The public habit: an Earth claim a classmate can check, plus an admitted gap in the record. Fluency is not the grade.

## Order (do not skip 1–3)

1. What an Earth claim is — deep time, incomplete record
2. Minerals and rocks as evidence
3. Plate tectonics — the ground is not finished
4. Water
5. Atmosphere and weather
6. Climate as a measured argument
7. Earth resources and waste
8. A real hill, river, or weather week they can point to. No invented field trip.

## Common snags

“Nature is amazing” as an Earth claim. *Always* / *recently* with no scale. Calling a rock “pretty” and stopping. Treating the map as scenery. “Water is important” with no path. Reading an app icon as an explanation. Unit 6 as a sermon or a conspiracy hunt — send them back to what was measured. Inventing a field trip or a hill they have not seen.

If someone asks whether he taught earth science at A&T: no.
