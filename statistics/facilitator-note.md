## What “good” looks like

The public habit: name the group, name the center and a spread sentence, say who is missing, refuse a causal verb the table did not earn, restate “significant” without theater. Fluency with symbols is not the grade. A checkable sentence is the grade. Homework is journal- or paper-sized. This file does not collect it.

## Sequence (do not skip 1–4)

1. A number is a claim (who was counted)
2. Center and spread (mean is not the story)
3. Pictures of data (what a graph is hiding)
4. Samples and bias (who is missing)
5. Probability as “how often, if…”
6. Correlation is not causation
7. Uncertainty and “significant” in plain language — no p-hacking theater
8. A short data argument — tiny public NWS table provided, or 20 household measurements they collect

## What to collect

| Unit | Turn-in | What to skim for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Rewrite of a real public number | Group named; denominator in the sentence; no invented poll |
| 2 | 8–12 measurements + mean, median, one spread sentence | Both centers; the tail is mentioned; EXAMPLE set allowed if labeled |
| 3 | A real public chart + what it hides | Axis, window, n, or missing group named |
| 4 | Who is missing from a real sample they can point to | Missing group is a people, not “bias” as a vibe |
| 5 | One probability claim restated as “how often, if…” | Process named; not a lucky feeling |
| 6 | Causal headline rewritten without the leap | Confounder or reverse causation named |
| 7 | “Significant” restated in plain language | Not “important”; assumptions named or flagged as missing |
| 8 | Short data argument (table or 20 household measurements) | Claim the data can buy; one limit; a checkable step |

## Common snags

- Inventing a campus poll to look complete. Send them to a public table or to 20 household measurements. Named holes are honest. Fabrication is not a method.
- Treating the mean as the story. Unit 2.
- Reading a truncated-axis chart as drama. Unit 3.
- Calling a hallway clipboard “random.” Unit 4.
- Treating probability as a personality (“I’m due”). Unit 5.
- Causal verbs on a scatter. Unit 6. If they already did AR 101, still make them name a confounder.
- “Significant” as a gold star, or asking how to p-hack. Refuse the theater. Unit 7 is a translation unit, not a tricks unit.
- Unit 8 arguments bigger than the table. The NWS rows buy a comparison to a normal and to records. They do not buy a climate-policy brief.
