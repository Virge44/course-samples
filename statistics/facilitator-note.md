# Facilitator note — Statistics 101

Draft sample. He did not teach a statistics department course. Do not apply.

## What not to claim

He did not teach statistics as a content expert. Lecturer courses were Critical Writing, Humanities, and Analytical Reasoning (University Studies, NC A&T, 2004–2016 — Analytical Reasoning at large enrollment). GRE/LSAT tutoring is reasoning muscle, not a stats appointment. As Director of Online Learning (2008–2012) he designed an online course called Scientific Development & Social Change — design/ops, not a science appointment. Do not invent labs, credentials, or “I taught Statistics at A&T.” Do not introduce him as a statistician.

## Pairing with Analytical Reasoning 101

This is the data sibling, not a remake. AR 101 already asks “percent of what?” and refuses a causal leap in ordinary talk. Do not paste AR’s dining-hall / shuttle spine into these files. Do not skip Units 1–4 because students “already know numbers.” Unit 6 is the trap they may already know, now in data clothes. If both samples are assigned, let students name the overlap in one sentence and then do the stats job.

## How to run it

Eight units plus the home. About 4–6 hours. Keep `styles.css` beside the HTML. Open `index.html` in a browser. Homework is journal- or paper-sized. This file does not collect it.

## What to collect

| Unit | Turn-in | Skim for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Rewrite of a real public number | Group named; denominator in the sentence; no invented poll |
| 2 | 8–12 measurements + mean, median, one spread sentence | Both centers; the tail is mentioned |
| 3 | A real public chart + what it hides | Axis, window, n, or missing group |
| 4 | Who is missing from a real sample | Missing group is a people, not “bias” as a vibe |
| 5 | One probability restated as “how often, if…” | Process named |
| 6 | Causal headline rewritten | Confounder or reverse causation named |
| 7 | “Significant” restated | Not “important”; assumptions named or flagged |
| 8 | Short data argument | Claim the data can buy; one limit |

## Common snags

Inventing a campus poll. Treating the mean as the story. Calling a hallway clipboard “random.” Causal verbs on a scatter. “Significant” as a gold star, or asking how to p-hack — refuse the theater. Unit 8 arguments bigger than the table.

If someone asks whether he taught Statistics at A&T: no.
