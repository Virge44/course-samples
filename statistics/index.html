Who this is for

First-year and gen-ed college students, mixed preparation. Adult tone. You do not need a statistics textbook and you do not need to love math. You need a habit: name who was counted, say what the center is hiding, and refuse a conclusion the table did not earn.

This sample sits beside Analytical Reasoning 101. That course treats a number in ordinary talk as a because that still has to clock in — percent of what, “studies show,” a causal leap. This course is the data sibling, not a remake. You will stay with small tables, labeled EXAMPLES, and one tiny public weather summary. You will not be asked to run software or to pretend this is a department methods sequence.

What the course asks you to do

A statistic is a public sentence. A private hunch with a decimal is not yet an argument. Each unit has three measurable objectives, a lesson, clickable practice with feedback, and one homework you could turn in. If you do the practices, plan on about four to six hours for the whole sample. A live semester would slow this down, add a computing lab, and give you more drafts. This file is the compressed 101: the spine, not the full term.

A number is a claim about a group. If you cannot name who was counted, you do not yet have a statistic. You have a costume.

Sequence

UnitTimeYou leave able to
1. A number is a claim
Who was counted		 ~40 min Rewrite a number so the group and the denominator are in the sentence.
2. Center and spread
Mean is not the story		 ~40 min Choose a center, and say in one sentence what it is hiding.
3. Pictures of data
What a graph is hiding		 ~40 min Name the scale, window, or missing piece a chart is using as an argument.
4. Samples and bias
Who is missing		 ~40 min Tell a sample from a population, and name a group the sample did not reach.
5. Probability as “how often, if…”
Not a lucky feeling		 ~40 min Restate a probability as a long-run frequency under a named process.
6. Correlation is not causation
The trap he already teaches		 ~40 min Name an association, a confounder, and a sentence that does not leap.
7. Uncertainty and “significant”
Plain language, no theater		 ~40 min Translate “significant” without treating it as important, proven, or a trick.
8. A short data argument
Tiny public table or 20 measurements		 ~45 min Make a claim a small table can actually buy, and name a limit.

How work is judged (if you have a section)

If an instructor used this sample in a live class, the standard would be public, not clever. Did you name the group? Did you give a center and a spread sentence? Did you say who is missing? Did you refuse a causal verb the table did not earn? Fluency with symbols is not the grade. The checkable step is the grade.

Homework lives in your own words. Paste the prompt into a journal, a discussion board, or a printed page. This file does not collect it. Do not invent a poll, a lab, or a school survey to look complete. Use a public table, the labeled EXAMPLE sets in these units, or twenty household measurements you actually take.
