Physics 101

A hallway is 28 meters if the tape says so. If it then says 19, the first number is wrong.

Self-paced No login No server First-year, mixed prep

That's the job. A number with a unit. Something you can check without a lab — a phone timer, a tape, a kitchen scale. No calculus. Don't open an outlet.

Eight sittings. Practice in the page. Homework you write elsewhere. About four to six hours if you do the practice. Course samples

Safety — once

Thought work and safe household looking only. Don't open outlets, panels, or appliance housings. Don't climb for a “better drop.” Don't heat a sealed container. Don't stand in a street to time a car. No weapons, no explosives, no “how to build.” Look at something you already use, from a place you already stand.

What you leave able to do

  1. Write a physical claim that names a quantity, a unit, and one look that would show the first number is wrong.
  2. Read a simple position–time or velocity–time graph, and compute a speed by hand.
  3. Name the forces in an everyday situation and say which of Newton’s three statements is doing the work.
  4. Track where energy goes in a household event — work and bookkeeping.
  5. Use momentum to describe a safe, everyday collision, including one hand calculation.
  6. Describe a sound with wave words (frequency, wavelength, amplitude) and one v = fλ calculation.
  7. Explain a lamp and a switch as a circuit job — charge, current, a complete path — without opening an outlet.
  8. Write a short look at one real, safe thing (a phone onto a bed, a kettle watched from a distance, a bike you already ride).
