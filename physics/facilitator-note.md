## How to run it

Keep the folder together. Point students at `index.html` opened in a desktop browser, not a Drive preview. Practices are in-page JavaScript. Nothing is uploaded. Homework is not stored in the files — collect it in your LMS journal, a shared folder, or on paper.

Sequence is the course. Eight units, in order. Each sitting: lesson a tired first-year can finish (about 600–900 words), clickable practice with feedback, one turn-in. Total student time if they do the practices: about four to six hours. This is a compressed conceptual 101, not a calculus sequence and not a lab course. If you stretch it across a term, use the units as weekly spines and add *your* assigned readings or your own supervised lab. Do not add “how to build,” weapons, or explosives content to these files. Do not treat the invented classroom sentences as student work or as data from a lab that ran.

Hand numbers the pages already use, labeled as approximations: near Earth, about 10 N of weight per kilogram of mass; free-fall speedup about 10 m/s each second if air is ignored; sound in room-temperature air about 340 m/s; typical U.S. household outlet about 120 V. Do not invent a more precise “class average” and paste it into the files.

## What to collect

| Unit | Turn-in | What to skim for (you do not need a rubric the first time) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Two rewritten claims + one paragraph | Quantity, unit, revision test. Flag vibes, slogans, missing units. |
| 2 | A real trip in numbers + one graph sentence | Speed with a unit. Velocity named with a direction. Graph slope used, not “the line goes up.” |
| 3 | One everyday scene, forces named | A pair (3rd), a keep-doing (1st), or a change-of-motion (2nd). Not “gravity is a theory.” |
| 4 | Energy bookkeeping of one household event | Where it went. Flag “energy disappeared” and “energy is all around us.” |
| 5 | A safe collision in numbers | p = mv once. No crash theater. No weapons. |
| 6 | One sound in wave words + one v = fλ | Frequency / wavelength / amplitude used as jobs, not as a vocab dump. |
| 7 | Lamp + switch as a circuit job | Complete path. Charge not “used up.” They did not open an outlet. |
| 8 | Investigation writeup, 250–400 words | One real observable they actually watched. Claim, measure, what would change their mind. No invented table. |

If there is no LMS, a shared doc per student is enough. Grade the public habit (a claim a measurement could refuse), not fluency-as-virtue and not “I like science.”

## Common snags

- Treating Unit 1 as a warmup and writing slogans anyway. Send them back. The claim type *is* the course.
- Unit 2 as a formula dump. They need to *read a graph*, not recite “rise over run” as a chant.
- Unit 3 becoming a biography of Newton. The three statements are tools. The apple story is a plaque.
- Unit 4 as “energy is life.” Bookkeeping or it does not count.
- Unit 5 turning into crash or “how hard could I hit.” Everyday, safe, no weapons.
- Unit 7 students opening an outlet or a charger. Stop. Observation of a closed, ordinary lamp only.
- Unit 8 inventing a tidy data table they did not measure. One real observable beats a fake lab.
- Students or reviewers asking if he taught physics at A&T. Say: no. Writing, humanities, reasoning. *Scientific Development & Social Change* was design/ops.

## What not to claim

Do not introduce the author as a physicist, a physics lecturer, or a software engineer. Do not call this official NC A&T curriculum or a live A&T section. ## Safety (non-negotiable)

Thought labs and safe household observation only. No weapons. No explosives. No “how to build.” No opening outlets, panels, or housings. No climbing for a drop. No heating a sealed container. No standing in a street to time traffic. If you add a live session, you own the safety briefing.

## Optional live / hybrid 20 minutes

Only if a cohort exists. Three volunteers read their Unit 1 rewritten claim. Class says whether it names a quantity, a unit, and a revision test. One Unit 2 graph read aloud (slope in words, then a number). Stop. Do not turn it into a problem-set blitz.

## Accessibility

Pages are keyboardable (skip link, buttons, textareas). Practices give feedback in-page, not only as color. Graphs have a text caption that states what the picture shows. If you need a no-JavaScript path, the homework prompts still function as writing assignments; the clickable practice will not.
